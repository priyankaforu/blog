---
title: "GPT Can Never Be My German Speaking Partner"
date: 2026-10-30
tags: ["personal", "life"]
---

I have been practising German on and off for the last 2 months, and I have a speaking partner who connects with me every evening, CET time. For the last week, he has been very busy, and I have been less motivated to learn those new nouns and verbs.

The best way to learn any new language is to live with it, to think in that language. And AI is so advanced now that it can even teach the language. We don't need any language teachers to help us with the grammar rules, right? I thought the same. I opened GPT for 3 days in a row to practise the language, started talking to it and asked it to correct me. On the fourth day, I was too lazy to repeat the drill, and the AI was never upset when I said no to it.

But my speaking partner is unlike GPT: I have to think about whether he will be upset. If I say I cannot make it today, he thinks that I am not committed to my goal. So I think twice before I say no to him. When I don't reach his level of language, I believe that I didn't learn at his pace, and I feel that I am not working hard enough to keep up. And of course, he is not a native German speaker, and neither am I. We make a lot of mistakes (I make more than him, though), and there is no one to correct us. Most of you might think we should not worry about that, because we have AI that can do anything, right? Yes, if we record our conversation, AI can correct it pretty well afterwards. But AI can never be my speaking partner, who gets upset, anxious, dull or angry whenever things don't work. Moreover, I can never say no to him, because I feel bad if he thinks that I don't care about my goal of learning German. That said, AI can never be the human I interact with, with all my emotions.
